The story

Why "Astraea"

Astraea was the goddess of purity and justice — the last immortal to walk among humans, and the one who refused to abandon truth as the world around her did. When she left, she became the constellation Virgo, still watching. We took her name because the supplement industry left truth behind a long time ago: blends that hide doses, labels that don't match contents, "tested" claims with nothing behind them. Astraea is our way of bringing her back down to earth — every batch tested by an independent lab, every result published. We don't ask you to trust us. We show you.

Voice & tone

Calm, exact, unafraid of numbers

We sound like a certificate, not an advertisement. We state facts and let them carry the weight. We never shout, never hype, never manufacture urgency. Confidence here looks like restraint.

We sound like

  • "Tested at 5.14% withanolides. Here's the report."
  • "No proprietary blends. Every ingredient and amount disclosed."
  • "If a batch fails, it doesn't ship."
  • Plain verbs. Specific numbers. Short sentences.

We never sound like

  • "MAX STRENGTH! CLINICALLY PROVEN MIRACLE!"
  • "Only 3 left — buy now before it's gone!"
  • Vague superlatives: "the best," "the purest," "#1."
  • Fake scarcity, countdown pressure, or invented reviews.

Compliance guardrails · read before writing any claim

Structure/function only. Never disease claims.

Supplements may describe how an ingredient supports normal body function. They may NOT claim to treat, cure, prevent, or diagnose any disease — that makes the product an unapproved drug and is illegal. Every marketing asset must follow this.

Allowed (structure/function)

  • "Supports a healthy stress response."
  • "Helps maintain muscle function."
  • "Supports normal immune function."
  • "Contributes to everyday energy."

Never (disease claims)

  • "Treats anxiety." / "Cures stress."
  • "Prevents COVID / colds / flu."
  • "Reverses aging." / "Heals your gut."
  • "Lowers blood pressure / cholesterol."
Required on all claim-bearing assets: the FDA disclaimer — "These statements have not been evaluated by the Food and Drug Administration. This product is not intended to diagnose, treat, cure, or prevent any disease." Route any claim you're unsure about to the regulatory reviewer before it ships. When describing testing, describe what we test and publish — never imply testing means the product treats anything.
