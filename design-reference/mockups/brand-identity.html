Design thesis

The label is a document, not an advertisement.

Supplement shelves shout. Astraea is designed like something you'd trust instead: the calm of archival paper, the precision of a lab certificate, and a night-sky mark that ties every product back to the myth.

Certificate, not carton

Layouts borrow from certificates of analysis — hairline rules, tabular data, monospace batch stamps. The transparency promise is visible in the typography itself.

One sky, two lines

A single label system covers all 26 SKUs. Starlight blue marks the Wellness line; ember marks Sport. Everything else stays identical, so the shelf reads as one brand.

Check the testing

The signature element: a QR strip on every bottle inviting the buyer to check the testing — it resolves to that exact batch's lab results, dates, and pass/fail data online, where it can stay current.

System rules

How it stays coherent at 26 SKUs.

Always

  • "Check the testing" QR strip on the front of every unit — never the back. Lot code inkjet-stamped on the base per FDA traceability rules.
  • Ink on paper: dark type, light ground, generous space.
  • Exact ingredient forms named on the front (bisglycinate, KSM-66, MK-7).
  • Verified green used only for testing data and COA links.
  • One constellation strip per product — its stars can encode the SKU number.

Never

  • No burst badges, "MAX STRENGTH" shouting, or gradient explosions.
  • No proprietary-blend language anywhere, ever.
  • No stock imagery of muscles, lab coats, or floating capsules.
  • No claim on the label that isn't on the COA.
  • No decorative use of the goddess figure — the sky stands in for her.
